3. Execution design & parameter search perfect signal · 6 rounds · all 100 days

Execution design

LayerWhat it does
signal → target positionthe target is a linear function of the signal, not a fixed size past a threshold
order sizeone fixed lot, not distance-to-target — a size that tracks the signal re-places on every event, and a re-place forfeits queue position
order managementsticky band, size capped by visible depth, level offset relative to the BBO
unwindingmarkets that leave the universe go reduce-only

Positions are sized in shares, not dollars. A share settles at $1 or $0, so N shares is $N of exposure at any price; sizing in dollars would make a 0.02 market 50× larger at equal risk.

The venue's median spread is exactly one tick — no room to quote inside it, so there is no push/pull layer: an order sits on the BBO or steps away from it.

Two designs rejected by measurement: inverse-volatility sizing (the σ estimate is reliable where it does not matter and unreliable where it does — it swings 10× at thin prices, and mid-range it is stable but worth only ±40%); freezing the target on a timer (profit-taking falls out of "target is a function of the current signal" — provided that function actually gets evaluated).

Search: fixing these parameters under a perfect signal

Feed the realized price move itself in as the signal — deliberate look-ahead. That decouples the execution ceiling from signal quality; the question becomes if the signal were perfect, which execution settings are right, and which of those conclusions transfer back to a real signal. Size comes first. 1× → 6× → 12× (lot 100/600/1200): total ×7.9 while per-unit edge rises, 514 → 555 → 563 bp. Adding size barely dilutes edge in this range — what binds first is the participation cap, not edge decay.

quote_offset_ticks: does not transfer. Under a perfect signal, resting one level back is pure loss — removing it multiplies the total by 2.4 ($304,611 vs $126,927). Yet bp is higher with it (578 vs 555): stepping back does raise per-unit edge, by far too little to repay the two-thirds of volume it gives up. Under a real signal, adverse selection costs much more — the offset stays.

sticky_ticks: transfers. At 0 (re-place on every BBO move) it does worse than baseline while orders go 1.57M → 3.23M. The cost of chasing has nothing to do with signal quality — this conclusion holds under the real signal too.

Open: lean_per_lot, clear_frac, flat_hours_to_end not yet searched; participation not computed per round — O2 fills 109k times and almost certainly breaches the 2% cap, so these totals cannot be read as attainable.
